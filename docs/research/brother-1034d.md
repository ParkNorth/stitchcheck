## Unit / naming checks

- Model number vs anything it implies: "1034D" carries no spec meaning. "D" is not "differential" in Brother's naming, although the machine has differential feed. Amazon refurbished titles add "Heavy-Duty", which is a listing word.
- Throat measured needle-to-body or including the harp height? Not published; sergers are not marketed on throat.
- Speed: manufacturer max 1,300 spm; retailer copy matches.

## Owner themes (paraphrase + attribute)

1. Threading and tension learning curve: owners describe rethreading many times before a chain forms; the fix is usually the threading order (lower looper, upper looper, right needle, left needle) and small single-dial tension changes. (owner: https://www.justanswer.com/small-appliance/ofjfq-its-brother-1034d-serger-rethreaded-25.html ; https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/127602)
2. Longevity varies: one owner in a Juki-vs-Brother thread reports about three years of moderate use before failure; another reports sixteen years on the same model. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/118297)
3. Noise and vibration at speed; a rubber mat under the machine is the standard remedy. (owner: https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/brand-new-brother-lock-1034d-noise-t210735.html)

## Cross-shop set

1. `brother-1034dx`: same stitch specs; DX adds LED, deeper trim trap, right-side dials, usually at a higher price.
2. `juki-mo-654de`: the usual step-up with 2-thread stitches, 1,500 spm and a heavier build.
3. `singer-14t968dc`: buyers wanting coverstitch compare a 1034D plus a coverstitch machine against one combo.

## Editorial angle

For a first-serger buyer deciding whether the lowest-priced 3/4-thread machine with differential feed is enough, or whether 2-thread and build quality justify the Juki premium.
