## Unit / naming checks

- Model number vs anything it implies: "2000CPX" is a tier code; "2000" is not a speed. The 1000CPX shares the 3-needle layout; the 900CPX has 2 needles. Top cover stitch is not on this model.
- Throat: Janome publishes the bed as 4 x 5.5 in "sewing area", not a needle-to-body throat.
- Speed: Janome 1,000 spm; dealers match.

## Owner themes (paraphrase + attribute)

1. Skipped stitches: reported by some, absent for others; fresh ELx705 needles and, in one thread, oiling the machine when new resolved it. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/105008 ; https://sewing.patternreview.com/review/machine/6869)
2. Tunneling on thin knits is handled with stabilizer tape and slightly looser looper tension rather than settings changes. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/review/machine/6332)
3. Long-term owners report a decade of reliable hemming; the tension release lever is the practical gain over the 1000CPX. (owner/blog: https://lladybird.com/2018/01/17/machine-review-janome-coverpro-2000cpx/ ; https://www.thelaststitch.com/janome-coverpro-2000-review/)

## Cross-shop set

1. `brother-2340cv`: cheaper dedicated coverstitch; pay more here for bed, free arm and tension release.
2. `singer-14t968dc`: combo alternative for buyers who would rather convert one machine.

## Editorial angle

For a knit sewist who hems often and needs to decide whether bed space, a free arm and a tension release lever are worth roughly $200 to $300 over the Brother 2340CV.
