## 1. Serger vs sewing machine

- A serger (overlock machine) "trims, stitches and overcasts the fabric edge at the same time"; a sewing machine joins pieces with straight, zigzag, decorative stitches and buttonholes. (https://www.ageberry.com/serger-vs-sewing-machine/)
- Thread count: a sewing machine has one spool pin (plus bobbin); a serger has "anywhere from four to eight" thread sources; serger uses 3 to 8 threads for an overlock stitch. (https://madamsew.com/blogs/sewing-blog/serger-versus-sewing-machine; https://fypower.org/serger-vs-sewing-machine/)
- Stitch type: serger makes an overlock stitch; most sewing machines make a lockstitch, some a chain stitch. (https://madamsew.com/blogs/sewing-blog/serger-versus-sewing-machine)
- Speed: sergers 1,300 to 1,500 spm vs sewing machines 300 to 800 spm (typical home machines; TL/PQ straight-stitch machines run 1,500). (https://mariasew.com/blog/serger-vs-sewing-machine/; https://www.worldweidner.com/pages/juki-tl-series-comparison-chart)
- Loopers replace the bobbin: "the upper looper and lower looper are used instead of a bobbin to form the stitches." Sequence: needle forms a loop; lower looper passes left to right through it carrying lower thread; upper looper passes right to left picking up lower looper and needle threads; needle descends behind upper looper to lock. (https://www.goldstartool.com/coverstitch-overlock-loopers/; http://trashn2tees.blogspot.com/2017/02/how-serger-actually-works.html)
- Knife: a cutting knife near the loopers trims the fabric edge as the stitch forms; Brother 1034D/DX have a retractable knife. (http://trashn2tees.blogspot.com/2017/02/how-serger-actually-works.html; https://www.sewingmachinefun.com/brother-1034d-vs-1034dx/)
- A serger can only sew along edges and cannot replace a sewing machine; the two are complementary. (https://fypower.org/serger-vs-sewing-machine/; https://www.ageberry.com/serger-vs-sewing-machine/)

## 2. Coverstitch vs serger

- Serger overlocks and trims; coverstitch "beautifully finishes hems ... especially knits", recognizable by two lines of top stitching, has no blade, cannot trim or overlock. (https://sewingmachinesplus.com/blogs/news/overlocker-serger-vs-coverstitch-machine-whats-difference; https://seamwhisperer.com/serger-coverstitch/)
- Threading: serger threads three or four paths including two loopers; coverstitch has two (or three) needle threads plus one looper thread underneath. (https://meissnersewing.com/blogs/inspiration/sewing-machine-vs-serger-vs-coverstitch)
- Needle positions: most coverstitch machines sew with one, two or three needles plus a looper. One needle = chain stitch (usable for topstitching jeans, binding, hemming). Two needles: remove left or right for a narrow twin row; remove the center needle for the wide row. Three needles = triple coverstitch, third row locks the looper thread, less stretch. (https://www.seamwork.com/sewing-tutorials/coverstitch-machines; https://mellysews.com/how-to-use-a-coverstitch-machine/; https://threadsmonthly.com/what-is-a-coverstitch-machine/)
- Use: narrow coverstitch pairs with a binding attachment for binding and fold-over elastic; 2-needle wide is the most versatile hem; the T-shirt hem is a coverstitch. (https://www.peekaboopatternshop.com/our-blog/coverstitch-101-tips-and-tricks-for-beginners; https://mellysews.com/sewing-machine-vs-serger-vs-coverstitch-whats-difference/)
- Combo machines exist (Juki MO-735 2/3/4/5 thread with chain and coverstitch; Singer 14T968DC; Bernina L 890). (https://www.allbrands.com/categories/267/printer_friendly; https://shop.berninausa.com/overlocker/L-series/BERNINA-L-890)

## 3. Mechanical vs computerized

- Mechanical: stitch length, width and tension set by dials and levers; no computer. Computerized: internal computer controls stitch selection and functions via buttons or touchscreen; features such as needle threader, thread cutter, auto tie-off, speed slider. (https://www.bsewinn.com/blogs/inspiration/computerized-sewing-machines-vs-mechanical; https://www.premierstitching.com/blog/computerized-sewing-machine-guide/)
- Inside: mechanical machines rely on gears, belts, shafts, springs, hooks a technician can inspect directly; computerized machines add circuit boards, sensors, displays, and separate stepper motors (one moves the needle bar for zigzag, often another drives the thread cutter). (https://rockymountainsewing.com/sewing-machine-repair-cost-guide/; https://sewingmachineadvice.com/what-is-a-computerized-sewing-machine/)
- Stitch count: entry computerized models ~39 stitches, advanced 600+; mechanicals fewer. (https://www.montavillasewing.com/blog/the-pros-cons-of-computerized-vs-mechanical-sewing-machines)
- Repair cost: mechanical repairs around $75; computerized over $100; board or screen failure can exceed the machine's price; manufacturers may stop making boards after a few years. (https://sewingtrip.com/sewing-machine-repair-cost-estimate/; https://www.bsewinn.com/blogs/inspiration/cost-of-sewing-machine-repair; https://thesimplefolk.co/blogs/guides/comparing-home-sewing-machine-types-mechanical-vs-computerized-features-to-consi)
- Same-brand price gap example: Singer 4432 mechanical $239.99 vs Singer 6800C computerized $379.99 (both "Heavy Duty" series). (https://www.michaels.com/product/singer-4432-heavy-duty-sewing-machine-355321199899361297; https://www.michaels.com/product/singer-6800c-heavy-duty-sewing-machine-355704209807409157)

## 4. What is a serger

- Definition: a sewing machine that "sews, trims and finishes a seam all at one time", using 3 to 5 threads (home machines); encases the raw edge in thread; valuable on knits because the stitch stretches; cannot replace a domestic sewing machine. (https://thequiltshow.com/quiltipedia/what-is-a-serger; https://meissnersewing.com/blogs/inspiration/everything-about-sergers)
- Thread configurations: 1-thread end-to-end seaming (industrial); 2-thread edging and seaming on knits and wovens, flatlock, elastic and lace, hemming; 3-thread one needle, lighter seams, narrow rolled hems, decorative edges; 4-thread two needles, "mock safety stitch", strength plus stretch for knits and medium wovens; 5-thread safety stitch = 2-thread chain stitch plus 3-thread overlock, two needles, for denim, canvas, upholstery. (https://www.sewingmachinesales.co.uk/faq/overlocker-stitches-explained/; https://www.sewingmachinesales.co.uk/faq/difference-between-4-thread-and-5-thread-overlocker/; https://sewingtrip.com/types-of-overlock-stitches/)
- Differential feed: the serger has front and back feed dogs that can move at different rates. 1.0 neutral; below 1.0 (0.7 or 0.6) front dogs slower, stretches fabric slightly (lettuce edge); above 1.0 up to 2.0 front dogs faster, prevents wavy knit seams and gathers lightweight fabric. (https://www.ageberry.com/serger-differential-feed/; https://www.simplesimonandco.com/2014/06/understanding-differential-feed-sergers.html/; https://weallsew.com/what-is-differential-feed/; https://sewinwithjuki.com/2026/04/18/what-is-differential-feed/)
- Rolled hem: very narrow 2- or 3-thread edge on lightweight fabric; 3-thread version uses one needle and both loopers and is on practically all sergers; made by retracting the stitch finger (rolled hem lever) so the edge rolls under while stitching; used on napkins, scarves, ruffles. (https://www.sewdaily.com/sewing/serger-school-3-ways-hem/; https://mygirlfriendsquiltshoppe.com/blogs/serger-smart/how-to-serge-a-rolled-hem-a-3-thread-rolled-edge; https://weallsew.com/overlocker-for-beginners-fast-finishing-with-rolled-hems/)
- Speed: Brother 1034D/DX 1,300 spm; Juki MO-654DE 1,500 spm; Janome AirThread 2000D 1,300 spm; Bernina L 850 1,500 spm. (https://sergerpro.com/brother-1034d-vs-1034dx-review/; https://jackmansfabrics.com/products/juki-mo-654de; https://www.janome.com/product/airthread-2000d/; https://shop.berninausa.com/overlocker/L-series/BERNINA-L-850)

## 5. Thick fabric (search budget ran out before dedicated queries; partial)

- Features named for thick fabric: "a powerful motor and a rock-solid frame", "high presser foot lift, which gives extra clearance under the needle", a motor that will not stall through layers of denim or canvas, "a walking foot (or at least good feed dogs)". (https://www.bsewinn.com/blogs/inspiration/sewing-machine-for-thick-fabric)
- Walking foot function: "feeds top and bottom layers together so thick seams don't shift or jam" (Singer 4452 ships with one). (https://www.singer.com/pages/best-heavy-duty-sewing-machine-models)
- Industrial machines: built for continuous use, stronger motors, metal internals, up to 5,500 spm, usually straight stitch only, mounted in a table. (https://sewingpanel.com/best-industrial-sewing-machines-for-home-use/; https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/101207)
- Domestic "heavy duty" claim examples: Singer 44xx "metal interior frame, stronger motor, 1,100 spm"; Brother ST371HD includes a "nonstick foot" for leather, suede and vinyl; ST531HD adds a metal needle plate. (https://sewingmachinesplus.com/blogs/news/best-heavy-duty-sewing-machine-buyers-guide-value; https://www.brother-usa.com/products/st371hd)

## 6. How to choose a quilting machine (partial)

- Throat space is "the area between the needle and the machine body"; larger throat lets you handle bigger quilts with less rolling. (https://www.bsewinn.com/blogs/inspiration/best-quilting-machine-for-beginners)
- Throat figures seen, for a tier table: Singer 6800C 6.4 in (Walmart title); Baby Lock Jubilant 6.25 in; Bernina 7 Series 10 in; Bernina 8 Series 12 in; Baby Lock Jazz II 12 in; Baby Lock Allegro 12 in; Janome Continental M8 13.5 in ("largest ... for a standalone sewing machine"). Whether each is measured needle-to-body is not stated. (https://www.walmart.com/ip/Singer-6800C-Heavy-Duty-Computerized-Sewing-Machine/1255013631; https://www.qualitysewing.com/products/babylock-jubilant; https://www.maggieframes.com/blogs/embroidery-blogs/bernina-comparison-chart-decode-models-find-your-perfect-sewing-machine; https://babylock.com/jazz-iid; https://rockymountainsewing.com/best-quilting-machines-for-home-quilters-in-2026/)
- Stitch regulation: Janome Continental M8 has an "Accurate Stitch Regulator (ASR) for free motion quilting"; Bernina BSR exists but was not re-sourced this session. Explanation of how regulators work: not found. (https://rockymountainsewing.com/best-quilting-machines-for-home-quilters-in-2026/)
- Other decision features cited: needle up/down, adjustable speed, walking foot, foot compatibility (feet not interchangeable across brands), extension table, dealer purchase. (https://www.diaryofaquilter.com/how-to-choose-a-sewing-machine/; https://www.gathered.how/sewing-and-quilting/quilting/how-to-choose-a-sewing-machine-for-quilting; https://madamsew.com/blogs/sewing-blog/quilting-sewing-machine)
- Longarm cost: entry ~$5,000, mid $10,000 to $20,000, high end $30,000+; used short/mid arm from ~$1,000; Pro-Stitcher computerization ~$10,000. (https://sadieraesquiltshop.com/how-much-is-a-long-arm-quilting-machine/; https://www.accomplishquilting.com/what-does-a-longarm-quilting-machine-cost.html; https://www.alandacraft.com/2020/03/20/how-much-are-quilting-machines/)

- Common beginner picks and why: Brother CS6000i (start/stop button, presser-foot alarm), Brother XR3774 (budget, many feet), Singer 4452 (mechanical, "impossible to break"), Juki HZL-LB5020 (speed control, needle up/down). (https://sewcanshe.com/beginner-sewing-machines-check-out-my-top-7-faves/; https://melaniekham.com/best-sewing-machine-for-beginners/)
