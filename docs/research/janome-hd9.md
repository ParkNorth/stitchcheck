## Unit / naming checks

- "HD9" is a model name. It is not a 9 in throat: Janome's own wording is "nearly 9 x 6 in" workspace, so the figure is soft and the 6 in is height, not depth. "Professional" is a name suffix, not a rating.
- Throat measured needle-to-body or including the harp height? Not stated. Not published as a number.
- Speed: manufacturer 1,600 spm; dealers repeat 1,600.
- Version: the original HD9 Professional (V1) is described by dealers as discontinued; current SKU is HD9 V2 (also a Black Edition, HD9V2BE). V2 uses a smaller proprietary jumbo bobbin and full bobbin case; V1 used an MB-size bobbin with a half-moon case. Parts are not interchangeable.

## Owner themes (paraphrase + attribute)

1. The straight stitch is clean and consistent; owners buy it for jeans and thick layers and call it one step below an industrial. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/review/machine/6724; https://sewing.patternreview.com/review/machine/6855)
2. The base box needs several hundred dollars of Janome accessory kits to match a Brother out of the box; the kits are called overpriced. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/review/machine/7134)
3. Dealer support and the V1/V2 bobbin split come up when buying online or used. (owner: https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/110451)
4. A bag-making blog with about a year of use reports layered vinyl and foam handled well and heavy-thread topstitching as the strong suit. (owner blog: https://www.applegreencottage.com/janome-hd9-sewing-machine-review/)

## Cross-shop set

## Editorial angle

For a bag maker or quilter deciding between the Janome HD9 and the Juki TL class; settles what the Janome premium buys (1,600 spm, 11 lb foot pressure, dealer support, jumbo bobbin) and what it does not (a thread trimmer, a published throat figure, feet in the box).
